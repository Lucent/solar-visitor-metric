# solar-visitor-metric — agent & architecture guide

The craft doctrine is the one that governs Ptable and Ptree (`../Ptable/STYLE.md`, `../Ptable/AGENTS.md`, `../Ptree/AGENTS.md`): single-developer craft, design by elimination, fix causes not cases, trust internal invariants and let impossible states crash loudly. This project is Python and science rather than a browser app, so the DOM/CSS rules do not apply; everything about honesty, deletion, and root causes does.

## Mission

Quantify how surprising a visitor's path is, as bits against an explicit null, so a planned path (close passes of several planets, a stellar itinerary) would stand out and a natural one would not. A score is only as good as its null: **every score must be a calibrated p-value.** Under the null, P(score ≥ x) must not exceed 2⁻ˣ; conservative is acceptable, overstating is a bug. Any change to a score or its null reruns the calibration checks (tests and the scripts' calibration output) before the change is trusted.

## Map

- `constants.py` — units and the known visitors' speeds.
- `planets.py` — the planet score: two-body hyperbolas against the planets' osculating orbits, closest approaches, the joint null over orientation and timing, the three-pass statistic and its calibration, a score's split into where its bits come from, and planned tours.
- `route.py` — the route score: straight asymptotes against linearly moving stars, synthetic or real (Gaia DR3, Hipparcos for the stars Gaia lacks), each field complete for every star that could meet a visitor within the hop limit.
- `real.py` — the real visitors: SBDB orbits, Horizons planets, the N-body path the site draws, the asymptotes the route uses, the planet score, and the comets that measure each era's survey bias.
- `simbad.py` — readable star names for the site, from SIMBAD by catalog designation.
- `svg.py` — plain-SVG figures for `route_check.py`, light colors inline with a dark-mode stylesheet.
- `real_visitors.py` — the visitors, their planned reference routes, the header's missions, and `site/data.json`.
- `route_check.py`, `kinematic_check.py`, `falloff_check.py`, `together_check.py` — the route's calibration on a synthetic field, under stellar kinematics, its hop falloff and floor by power, and planets and route calibrated jointly.
- `statistic_check.py` — how to combine the planets: candidate statistics' power on random planned tours, and the visitors under each statistic and under alternative planet nulls.
- `site/` — the GitHub Pages explorer: `index.html` (shell and stylesheet), `app.js` (three.js scene, Astronomy Engine planets, panels), `dark.js` (Ptree's theme toggle), and `data.json`. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes the folder on every push that touches it.

## Decisions

- **Closeness is geometry.** A visitor that can steer at all reaches any planet with a correction of meters per second made years out, so what a planned visit leaves behind is the passes themselves. Paths are two-body hyperbolas about the Sun and planets move on their osculating orbits; planetary gravity plays no part in the score.
- **The planet null randomizes what a planner chooses and keeps what discovery fixes.** Null visitors share the visitor's speed and perihelion distance (discovery strongly favors small perihelia), and take a uniformly random orientation and a perihelion time anywhere in 10,000 years of the planets' motion on one clock. A path in the planets' plane and timed to meet several of them therefore scores for both, and Earth counts like any other planet: discovery favors a close Earth pass, and so would a visitor interested in life.
- **Planet score: one p per planet, the three smallest combined, calibrated by its own null.** A planet's p is the chance a null visitor passes it at least this close, from one sample of null visitors; below the closest hundred, a straight pass's area law, d². The statistic is the product of the three smallest p's, so one very close pass dominates it and two or three moderate ones compound. Every candidate statistic is valid once calibrated; this one detects random planned tours best (`statistic_check.py`), and the statistic is chosen by that power, never by the visitors' scores. Its distribution comes from an independent sample of null visitors, so the planets' dependence on one shared path is built in; a statistic few null visitors reach is scored by the count's 95% upper confidence limit, and planned tours draw tens of millions of null visitors to reach theirs.
- **A planet score opens into where its bits come from.** The parts are steps that each narrow the null, so their bits add exactly to the whole: what survey bias explains, then lying as close to the ecliptic as the path does, then pointing as it does within that tilt, then arriving when it did on the path itself. Survey bias is measured, not assumed: long-period comets arrive from nearly every direction, so the planes of those first seen within five years of a visitor show how much more often its era's surveys found paths at each tilt, and the null reweighted by that excess gives the bias its bits. Its bands are wide enough to hold several of an era's comets, since an empty band would wrongly give in-plane arrivals no weight. Only the bias is an explanation; lying in the plane is a coincidence of direction, the one a planner touring the planets would choose, and is not labeled otherwise.
- **Route: exact per-star p, noise convolved, near meetings weighted.** Per star, the exact chance an isotropic visitor at this speed passes this close, convolved with the Rice distribution of the measured miss (otherwise noise below a barely reachable star's floor scores as impossible). The chance is the solid angle of the miss seen from the meeting, so it measures the aim's precision, the same for any star at the same angle; how much a star is worth trying is a separate weight. Look-elsewhere is a weighted Bonferroni with weights hop⁻⁴, hops under 2 pc counted as 2 pc, among stars reachable at this speed: each doubling of hop gets half the share of the one before, nearly equal hops get nearly equal shares, and no one star met near the Sun takes the budget. Shallower falloffs spread it over the thousands the catalog cannot place; the falloff and floor are chosen by power on planted routes (`falloff_check.py`), never by the visitors' scores. Reachability and hop depend only on speed, so restricting to them keeps the test valid.
- **A route is one itinerary: origin, Sun, destination.** The arrival end is scored against isotropic arrivals; the departure end given the arrival, where the Sun's turn angle is taken as observed (it is set by the perihelion) and only its orientation about the arrival direction is random. The two p's are then independent by construction and combine exactly, and a pair of stars on one line through the Sun is not counted twice. The route p's assume isotropic arrivals; `kinematic_check.py` redraws them from the catalog's stars moving at the visitor's speed, which is how interstellar objects actually arrive, and the score must stay calibrated there too. Rerun it whenever the route score or the catalog changes.
- **A hop is where the meeting happens, not where the star is today.** Candidates are the stars a visitor at v∞ could meet within 20 pc of the Sun. Stars outrun slow visitors, so most candidates are farther than 20 pc now; a field is complete for visitors faster than 20 km/s when it holds every star within 20 pc × (1 + |w| / 20 km/s) today. Convolution is spent only on stars whose lower bound on p is below their weight, since no other star can set the score.
- **Planets and route together.** Their p's combine as independent, p₁p₂(1 − ln p₁p₂), so looking in two places is paid for. Both nulls randomize the arrival direction, so `together_check.py` scores each null visitor for planets and route on one path and the combination must stay calibrated; rerun it whenever either score changes.
- **A bad radial velocity fakes an encounter.** Real proper motion plus a spurious radial speed of hundreds of km/s puts a star's track almost through the Sun. Gaia velocities count only with `rv_expected_sig_to_noise` ≥ 5 (Katz et al. 2023) and a Galactic rest-frame speed under 600 km/s, above the Galaxy's escape speed.
- **Hipparcos fills what Gaia cannot see.** Gaia saturates on the brightest stars and measures radial velocities only for bright, cool enough ones, which leaves out the neighbors everyone knows (Sirius, Vega, α Cen A). Hipparcos (van Leeuwen 2007) with Pulkovo radial velocities (Gontcharov 2006) supplies them, carrying its own larger errors. The two are matched by position at the Gaia epoch, not by Gaia's cross-match table, which misses about 800 pairs; a Hipparcos star within 2″ of a usable Gaia star is that star.
- **A star goes by the name a reader knows.** SIMBAD supplies it: a proper name, else a Bayer, Flamsteed or variable-star name, else a discoverer's catalog (Ross, Wolf), else a Gliese number, else the catalog designation; survey acronyms read no better than the designation.
- **The site draws one law.** Every distance is drawn at asinh(r / r₀): to scale inside the zoom radius, logarithmic beyond, so a planetary flyby and a star 100 pc away share one view, and zoom changes r₀ rather than the camera. The time slider runs the same function. The picture is a pure function of visitor, time and zoom; the radios and ranges are the state, and the panels are built once. The Python owns every number; the page reads `data.json` and only places things (Astronomy Engine supplies planet positions, valid for millennia, so planets hide beyond 3,000 years from now).
- **The page is the one scroller.** The panel runs its full length and the view with its controls stays pinned beside it, within its own grid cell. An inner scroller beside an animating WebGL canvas never passes its scroll on to the page in Chrome, so no part of the page scrolls on its own.
- **Every visitor is shown beside missions scored by the same tests.** The header flies three missions at 3I's speed among the planets as they were for 3I: a tour planned to pass Earth, Mars and Jupiter, a route from Sirius to α Cen A, and that route's own path timed to pass Earth and Mars; a part a mission does not aim at scores nothing. Each visitor's panel adds a route planted at its own speed, from the nearest star it can reach to the next. Routes are planted on the real catalog: the planned path passes through each star, as a visit would, and is seen through the catalog's own errors; a synthetic field or better precision than the catalog's would flatter the yardstick.

## Conventions

- **Tabs**, per `.editorconfig`. Comments explain why. **No hard line breaks in prose** — docstrings, comments, and docs are one line per paragraph or bullet.
- **No speculative error handling.** Validate only external data at its boundary (SBDB, Horizons, Gaia responses); trust internal invariants.
- **Generated output is regenerated, never hand-edited.** `out/` belongs to the scripts; `data/` is a fetch cache. Neither is committed. `site/data.json` is the published result and is committed, so Pages serves exactly what the scripts produced.
- **The site is no-build and framework-free:** an import map to the latest three.js and Astronomy Engine, newest CSS (`light-dark()`, registered colors the WebGL scene reads back), no fallbacks or shims. The user performs all visual inspection; never run a browser or browser automation.
- **AGENTS.md records decisions and operating instructions, not results; the README presents the findings.** When a number in the README changes, rerun the script that produced it and update the README in the same commit; when a doc and the code disagree, fix the doc.
- Python packages only in the project `.venv`. Parallel scripts keep their work under `if __name__ == "__main__"` (Python 3.14 starts workers with forkserver).
- Commits are atomic: one logical change with its tests and doc lines.
